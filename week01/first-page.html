A poor widow named Naliaka lived on the edge of a village, on a strip of land too steep and too dry for anyone else to want. She had no husband, no sons grown enough to work, and no goats. What she had was one cow — thin-ribbed, patient-eyed, the color of rust — and that cow was everything. It gave the milk she sold in the market, the milk that bought maize meal and school exercise books for her youngest daughter, the milk that stood between the household and hunger.
